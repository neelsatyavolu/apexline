APEXLINE

Privacy

Apexline counts installs and page views anonymously so the developer knows how many people use it. It has no ads, no cookies and no personal tracking.

The app

Once a day, Apexline sends an anonymous usage ping to analytics.n3el.dev, a stats service run by the Apexline developer. The ping contains:

  • a random install ID created on your Mac (not linked to you or your accounts)
  • the Apexline version, macOS version and chip type
  • whether it is a release or development build

The service also notes your country from the connection. Your IP address is not stored.

To turn it off, open Settings → Privacy and switch off Share anonymous usage stats. Apexline then sends nothing.

This website

apexline.io uses a small cookieless script from analytics.n3el.dev to count page views and download clicks. It records the page address (without query strings), the referring site, your country and a rough device and browser type. It sets no cookies, stores nothing in your browser and can't link your visits across days.

What is never collected

  • No names, emails, F1 TV or AI account details, passwords or tokens.
  • No IP addresses are stored, and no cookies are used.
  • Nothing about what you watch, your files or your settings.

Raw stats are deleted after about 13 months.

Other services the app talks to

  • F1 TV and Formula 1 live timing, when you sign in and watch. Your F1 TV and AI sign-ins stay on your Mac.
  • OpenF1 and news feeds, for timing, results and headlines.
  • ChatGPT (Codex) or Grok, only if you connect one for AI Copilot.
  • apexline.io, for update checks, the OpenF1 proxy, and friends and watch parties. Friends and watch parties store your display name, friend code and watch-party chat messages.

Questions

Apexline is open source, so you can read exactly what it sends on GitHub. Questions or requests: open an issue.
